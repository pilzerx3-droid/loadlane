# Customer interview guide (US shippers)

Goal: 10 to 15 conversations of 30 minutes to learn how transport tracking, documents, quotes and spot freight work today. You are researching, not selling.

## Who to talk to
Transportation or logistics managers at mid-size US manufacturers and distributors, dispatchers at 3PLs, and shipping or receiving coordinators. Look for 20 to 500 loads per week and many outside carriers. **Do not interview your employer or its contacts, and do not share any employer information.**

## Rules
- Ask about what people did last time, not what they might do.
- Do not pitch. Do not show the product until the last five minutes.
- Ask permission to take notes. Never ask for confidential data.
- Write down exact words and numbers right after the call.

## Script (use in English)
**Opening (2 min):** "Thanks for the time. I'm researching how teams keep track of their truck shipments. I'm not selling anything. Is it okay if I take notes?"

**Today's process (10 min)**
1. "Walk me through the last shipment that went wrong. What happened?"
2. "How do you find out where a truck is right now?"
3. "How many loads do you manage in a typical week? How many carriers?"
4. "Which tools do you use today (TMS, ERP, spreadsheets, email, phone)?"
5. "When a customer asks where their order is, what do you do?"

**Documents and quotes (8 min)**
6. "How do bills of lading and proof of delivery reach you? How long does it take?"
7. "When you need a price for a load, how do you get it? How long does that take?"
8. "How do you check whether you made money on a load?"

**Spot freight (5 min)**
9. "When you need a truck on short notice, what do you do? Who do you call?"
10. "How do you compare offers and keep a record of the decision?"

**Pain and budget (5 min)**
11. "What costs you the most time in this process every week?"
12. "What have you tried to fix it? What happened?"
13. "Who decides on new software, and what does the process look like?"
14. "What do you pay for tools like this today?"

**Closing (2 min)**
15. "Who else should I talk to? Would you be open to a short follow-up in a few weeks?"

## Outreach templates (English)
**LinkedIn connection note (under 300 characters):**
"Hi [Name], I'm researching how logistics teams track shipments and handle paperwork. Not selling anything. Could I ask you a few questions in a 30-minute call?"

**Follow-up message:**
"Hi [Name], thanks for connecting. I work in industrial logistics and I'm exploring how mid-size shippers deal with tracking, documents and short-notice freight. I'd love to learn from your experience in a 30-minute call, no pitch. Would [day] or [day] work? Happy to share what I learn afterwards."

## After 10 to 15 interviews
Count how many named the same pain, how many already pay for a tool, and how many would join a pilot. That decides which pillar we build first.
